# Architecture and extension guide

The game is a Vite application using JavaScript ES modules and Three.js. It builds one connected world at startup and runs one local campaign. The refactor separates reusable rules and data from browser-facing systems, but the runtime still uses shared singleton objects and several circular imports. Treat this document as a map of the current implementation, not a claim that it is a general-purpose game engine.

## Startup and frame ownership

`src/main.js` owns boot order. It awaits local PBR triplets and the face color asset, builds the sky/environment map, world and roof task fixtures, then resolves coincident structural surfaces. Custom roof finishes are applied after surface ownership; the bounded static interior diffuse bake follows. Generated/procedural wall maps and authored skin remain load-failure fallbacks. Player, weapons and floor supplies precede the ballistic index, which sees final world triangles before combat actors and pickup halos. Mission listeners, the eight-light pool, static room reflections, enemy pools and focused shadows follow. Character and held-weapon warmup finish behind the loading menu before the animation loop and start button are enabled. The player starts with **fists**, including after a full campaign reset; weapon pickups are earned from defeated armed enemies.

`renderer.setAnimationLoop(frame)` is the only gameplay frame driver. The `FixedStepClock` in `src/core/frame-budget.js` advances at **1/120 second**, with at most **eight simulation steps per rendered frame**. Excess time after a stall is bounded rather than replayed indefinitely. Pauses reset the accumulator; returning from a hidden tab does not advance missed gameplay time.

Within each simulation step, `main.js` advances `GameTime.elapsed`, weapon timers, player/input, enemies, the encounter director, triggers, healing, the final choice, endings, combat statistics, and HUD timers. Visual effects, weapon presentation, environment animation, objectives, and navigation then receive only the amount of simulation time that actually advanced. New cooldowns, deadlines, attack windups, and mission delays must use this clock and `dt`, not independent `setTimeout`, `setInterval`, or wall-clock comparisons.

The gamepad is polled even while menus are open so a controller can resume play. Simulation stops during pause, death, the briefing, a resolved ending, page hiding, or graphics-context loss. Rendering is also suspended while idle, except for explicit inspection or a final transition render. Graphics-context loss pauses the session and asks for a reload; there is no transparent world reconstruction.

Pure data/rule modules should remain leaves of the dependency graph: they must not import HUD, DOM input, scene construction, or renderer initialization. They are the preferred location for new state transitions that can be tested independently.

Runtime imports are not a strict hierarchy. Notable cycles include input/HUD, player/weapons, enemies/mission/weapons, and world/zone builders. Boot-time calls currently resolve these dependencies after module evaluation. Avoid reading a cyclic import's state during module initialization. Prefer an injected callback or a narrow event subscription, as with `onZoneChange`, when adding a new relationship. Extracting these remaining singleton dependencies is future work.

## Mission and spatial contracts

The authored route is `apartment → neighbor → balcony → stairwell → roof → scaffolding → street`, with the bakery as the eighth zone and one final arena. `ZONE_ORDER`, `CHECKPOINTS`, `ZONE_WAVE_CONFIG`, and `FINAL_ENCOUNTERS` live together in `game/mission-data.js`.

Checkpoint and spawn `y` values describe the **feet/floor position**, not camera height. `Player.pos` is an eye anchor; restore code adds the standing eye height and resets crouching, velocity, jump buffering, camera easing, and held inputs. Keep authored anchors on supported floors with sufficient standing clearance. Do not save the player's arbitrary trigger-entry position, which can be airborne.

`moveCapsule` resolves horizontal floor support before rounded-edge contacts can turn walking into an upward bounce. Previously grounded, nonascending bodies use a circular footprint and the existing 2.5 cm downward ground probe. A successful step can therefore remain supported by the leading part of the foot before the body centre crosses the tread edge. The support plane may not exceed the previous foot height; the separate step finder still owns upward movement and keeps its 0.30 m riser limit and full capsule clearance checks. This adds no ramp geometry and does not weaken walls, rails or ceilings.

Airborne bodies still need to cross a floor plane directly below their centre; footprint support cannot pull a jumping or falling body onto a neighbouring ledge. Grounded support ends when the circle no longer overlaps a surface. The overlap test must use a circle, not an expanded rectangle that invents support at diagonal corners. Small cracks within the footprint can be straddled; unsupported gaps still cause a fall. Camera step easing and walking bob remain separate presentation effects, and flush seams must not produce step offsets or false airborne frames.

Player movement and automatic stepping use the same accepted input. The input layer already applies the gamepad dead zone; do not add another movement-magnitude cutoff that leaves fine stick input moving into a riser with stepping disabled. Regression coverage must execute `playerUpdate` with its real acceleration, stance and grounded state, including slow analog commands and stopping/resuming on individual treads. Driving a test capsule with forced horizontal velocity alone does not reproduce these failures.

`world/zones/traversal.js` only re-exports the balcony, stairwell, roof and scaffolding builders. Each of those spaces has its own module. Change the shared layout before moving geometry, checkpoints, navigation targets or encounter pockets independently.

`STAIRS.roofThreshold` defines the physical joint between the top landing and the roof. Their floor faces meet at edges instead of overlapping at height 14 m. The east wall stops at the threshold underside, sill bands carry the upper jambs, and the roof parapet/coping meet the wall exterior without overlapping visible doorway faces. Do not add a new surface over the threshold to hide a gap; verify the actual indexed triangles, support connections and bidirectional movement when changing this joint.

`APARTMENT_DOORS` identifies the closed 4A entrance and open 4B terrace passage. Builders cut each opening once and `createDoorAssemblies` supplies both faces from the same descriptor. A closed entrance has one colliding leaf, never a door skin over an uncut wall or two independent leaves. The neighbor's linen cupboard is recessed interior furniture, not a second exterior entrance. Keep thresholds flush and exterior trim out of the usable gallery lane.

`createInteriorProps` accepts construction services rather than importing the runtime world. Fixtures retain one grounded solid body; sofas retain their original base/back/arm records. Cached rounded boxes, chamfered trim, profiled legs and attached piping replace visible surfaces without changing the apartment's 74 movement AABBs or 43 furniture support records. Small details still enter per-material decoration batches. Shared wood and linen maps also supply tinted upholstery; appliance enamel, hardware and opaque glazing keep their surface semantics. Dimensions are metres and fixture yaw is a quarter turn. Preserve dining/partition clearance and the real bullet gaps between chair parts. Test rendered support contacts, including the pillow/mattress and table legs, not only registry bounds. Environment props resolve named surfaces such as `apartment-kitchen-top` and `neighbor-dining-top`.

`createInteriorStoryDetails` supplies varied shelf books, framed landscapes, refrigerator notes, rugs, folded throws and compact kitchen groups through those same construction services. Cached geometry and shared opaque book/rug atlases avoid individual texture or update loops for each prop. The bakery preparation group resolves the actual `bakery-prep-island-top` mesh before placing its board, rolling pin, bowl and note. Bakery loaves use flattened bearing surfaces and scored crust geometry; paper packages share an atlas. A dedicated steel material replaces the preparation worktop/front finish without changing the shared outdoor metal material or the island's movement envelope.

`crt-housing.js` supplies the television's tapered opaque case, recessed vent backing, bezel, dials and feet. The apartment builder retains its console support, glass, glow and flicker light, with one body shadow caster. Ballistics use the actual sloped shell and recessed backing; the upper rear corners and vent depths are not approximated by the former rectangular case.

Selected structural boxes use `addBox(..., { architecture: { id, kind, supports, supportKind } })`. `Architecture.elements` records intended bounds, the built object, its collider if any, and declared supports. A `bearing` connection rests on or modestly overlaps a lower support; `anchored` members connect at an edge or through a joint; `ground` roots reach the ground; `suspended` members hang from a higher connection. Diagonal members and screens register through `structures.js`. Runtime QA checks these selected connections and visible/collider bounds. This is a game authoring contract, not a building-code or structural engineering simulation, and does not certify unregistered decoration.

`finalizeWorldSurfaces()` runs after **all** zones and surroundings have been built, before the first render. The street builder adds walls that support floors built earlier, so this cannot be finalized independently within a zone. The cached report lives in `WorldState.surfaceOwnership`; no clipping or geometry allocation runs per frame.

For static rectangular architecture, a floor, deck, slab, roof, landing, tread or ceiling owns its coplanar surface over a supporting wall, column, beam or building. An explicit `threshold` owns its flush strip within a floor. Equal-priority finishes use the stable architectural ID to choose one owner, independent of construction order. Only same-facing horizontal triangles compete, with a **0.000001 m** plane tolerance for float-buffer roundoff. Both upward wall caps and downward faces at ceiling joints are handled. Real height offsets, vertical faces, opposing bearing contacts and uncovered strips remain intact. A threshold placed above a floor is therefore not flattened into it.

The pass subtracts covered rectangles and clones only changed geometry. It preserves texture coordinates, face normals, complete index draw ranges, object transforms, materials, depth settings, shadow flags, structural bounds and colliders. Attribute values are decoded before copying, including normalized, interleaved and half-float buffers. New fragments remain in the original mesh. It does not use `renderOrder`, polygon offset, disabled depth testing, or overlapping replacement skins. Do not use a mesh bounding box as proof of rendered coverage after finalization: inspect the triangles referenced by its index buffer.

Eligibility is deliberately conservative: ordinary opaque built-in mesh materials on the world layer, a complete validated rectangular box, cardinal positive transforms and affine face UVs. Transparent, masked, displaced, clipped, custom-shader, non-world-layer, dynamic, door/gate, instanced and malformed inputs are skipped. Register new static structural surfaces with the appropriate kind; author physical seams for decorative trim and irregular meshes. Treat finalized geometry as immutable. A layout rebuild needs fresh authored geometry and a fresh finalization report, not repeated clipping of an edited finalized mesh. This is surface cleanup for the authored world, not a general CSG system.

`EncounterSchedule` owns pending contacts, spawned groups, clearance and retirement. The authored totals below are available rosters, not a promise that every player must defeat every contact before leaving a space. Each live cap applies to its encounter; survivors from earlier checkpoints can remain alongside later groups.

| Zone | Finite contacts | Maximum alive | Composition and pacing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Apartment | 2 | 2 | One brawler and one bat carrier; the player begins empty-handed. |
| Neighbor | 4 | 2 | An opening melee pair, then a gunman and bat carrier. |
| Balcony | 8 | 3 | Three forward melee pairs plus two additional rear contacts; at most one designated rear attacker. |
| Stairwell | 8 | 2 | Four pairs assigned to successive landings. |

For the balcony, `EncounterRouteProgress` measures distance along the terrace/gallery centreline and never rewinds during a retreat. Stage gates keep forward contacts ahead of the player. Holding position allows a breather centred on 4.5 seconds; advancing can stage the next pair after at least 1.25 seconds, while ordinary safe-spawn distance still applies. Gallery anchors alternate between z=0.62 and z=1.18 before bounded offsets. The two forward arrivals must satisfy both lateral and angular separation from the actual player position. `selectEncounterFrontPair` evaluates candidate pairs jointly, backtracking the first anchor when it prevents the second; an opposite-side rear attacker cannot satisfy this forward-pair contract. The director acquires both real rigs before consuming either slot, rolling back the first acquisition if the second fails. A checkpoint retry resets route and stage state. Fists do not produce a weapon pickup.

The balcony reserves entries 0 and 1 for each forward pair; entry 2 adds rear pressure in its second and third groups. The stairs retain their second-entry rear policy and two-contact cap. Stable `entryIndex` and simulation-only `waitedSeconds` survive deferred attempts. Balcony rear anchors must be on traversed ground; stair rear anchors belong to the lower landing of the current flight. Every actual rear arrival, including a stair forward slot the player has turned away from, is downgraded to fists against melee gear or an empty firearm and at most a bat against a firearm with loaded or reserve rounds. The authored weapon composition is a template, not a guaranteed drop roster.

Rear slots require the full 0.48 m radius, 2.02 m high spawn envelope to be outside the rendered camera view or concealed behind opaque structural boxes. A delayed forward fallback becomes eligible after 1.5 seconds on the balcony or 4.5 seconds on stairs, but it must still be concealed and pass all ordinary placement checks. One solid box must block all nine rays to the body corners and centre; rays hitting different slats cannot prove that the gaps between them hide the body. Hidden parents, masked or transparent materials and collision-only screens are not cover. No valid candidate means deferral, never an unchecked spawn. Successful arrivals get one copied investigation target and at least one second before attacks can begin. Their type, entry metadata and count are committed only after pool acquisition succeeds.

`selectSafeSpawn` enforces floor support, height tolerance, capsule clearance, distance from the player, and spacing from living enemies. A rejected candidate returns `null`; the director retries the pending enemy later. Never turn a failed safety check into an unchecked fallback spawn. This is especially important for stacked stairwell and scaffolding surfaces.

Ordinary groups wait for the previous group to clear before the recovery interval and next stage. On the balcony, `advanceOnFrontClear` allows the next pair to stage after the two forward enemies fall, even when an older rear contact is still alive or waiting for a safe arrival. `frontAliveByWave` and `rearAlive` keep those states distinct; full group-clear events and health rewards still require every contact to be defeated. Pending rear contacts remain in the finite roster. The roof deliberately differs: both opening sentries must spawn and be defeated before reserves activate. The reserve delay is centred on 1.75 seconds, with later groups centred on 4.5 seconds apart. Groups may overlap living survivors. Pending contacts, the total live cap and the enforcer cap still apply. No encounter repeats its final group indefinitely.

`EncounterSchedule(config, { seed })` samples one immutable `variation` plan. A null or omitted seed retains exact authored timing and candidate ordering for explicit fixtures. Runtime directors and finales ask `EncounterSeeds.next()` for a fresh uint32 on each attempt, including retries; zero is a valid fixed seed. Timing and placement use separate addressed hash streams keyed by encounter, wave and entry. Retrying a blocked candidate, failing a pool acquisition, pausing or resetting the same schedule cannot reroll its preferred pockets or delay samples. Live geometry and player movement can still change which candidate is safe; a fixed seed is not a replay of the entire simulation.

Ordinary first, recovery and reinforcement delays vary by up to 18%. Minimum recovery, stage handoffs, rear fallback/grace and finale deadlines remain unchanged. The balcony's opening delay may shorten from 0.1 seconds, but never lengthen beyond it, preserving the pair during an immediate sprint. `recoveryDelay` is the sampled full breather used to enforce minimum elapsed recovery; `timerDuration` is the duration that armed the current timer. Mission diagnostics expose these and the encounter seed.

`variedSpawnCandidates` shuffles only stage-eligible anchors and applies bounded horizontal offsets (up to 0.6 m per axis on the roof, with smaller offsets on narrow walkways). Every offset retains its original anchor as a fallback. Both must pass the same support, capsule, height, distance, route, crowding, concealment and front-pair bearing checks. The balcony opening gives its two exposed anchors a preferred tier, randomizing within it before considering older corner pockets. `capsuleHasClearance` checks the full arrival envelope; do not use the AI steering probe's reduced radius for this contract. Seeded retries retain their ordering instead of rotating it by retry count. Add safe authored pockets before expanding jitter; random coordinates are never a substitute for verified space.

Stairs and scaffolding gate groups by the player's foot height. Passing a stair landing or dropping below a scaffold platform retires only its unspawned contacts. Living enemies retain their slots under that encounter's live cap and remain threats until defeated. Stair departure requires grounded progress, so jumping on a landing cannot erase its encounter. Checkpoint crossings and final branch choices also preserve all living actors while stopping the old arrival schedule. The scheduler records skipped contacts separately from defeated ones and does not declare a wholly unspawned encounter cleared. Bypassing contacts grants no kills, drops or recovery reward; retry clears survivors and resets this state.

A checkpoint contains a zone, an authored anchor, the weapon/ammunition and armor snapshot, the ammo-cache ledger, and an optional final branch. Restore validates the anchor, clears transient enemies and drops, resets the director and ending state, restores player/loadout/supplies, and re-enters the correct encounter. Survivors from earlier checkpoints persist only within the current life and are not saved for retries. It is an in-memory session checkpoint, not a disk save or an exact replay of every world object.

Temporary armor drops retain their enemy's origin zone as metadata, but visibility and collection follow their physical location. Distance, floor separation and solid cover still prevent remote collection. A survivor defeated after crossing into another area leaves a usable vest there; checkpoint retries clear these drops with the rest of the previous life.

`HEALTH_SUPPLIES` declares named, fixed floor positions and amounts; `initMission` creates each mesh and light once. Both rooftop routes have two 30-HP packs. The north route passes west of the lightwell, then north of the mechanical house; its supplies are separate from the front route near the water tank. `ROOF_HEALTH_ROUTES` records landmark waypoints and sightlines for physical tests, independent of NPC positions. Full health preserves a pack; collection never exceeds 100 HP. Zone restoration reactivates the existing objects instead of allocating replacements. Inactive packs set their light-source intensity to zero and tag the source with its zone, since the light budget does not use source visibility as an availability signal. These positions and amounts do not randomize with encounters.

`createHealthPickupModel` supplies a shaped shell/lid, gasket, open carry handle, latches and top/side crosses. One case submits 704 triangles in three material draws per color pass; its cached geometry and three solid-color materials are shared, with no texture maps. Mission still owns identity, hover/yaw, halo, collection and restoration. The asset does not change the persistent HUD warning thresholds or healing behavior.

Three floor ammo boxes are declared in `AMMO_SUPPLY_CACHES`: `balcony-reserve`, `roof-west-reserve`, and `roof-east-reserve`. Each owns an independent 120-unit budget shared across firearm types. A round costs 5 units for a pistol, 20 for a shotgun, 4 for an SMG, or 3 for a machine gun. A box adds only accepted reserve rounds, with per-type reserve limits, and neither equips a weapon nor fills a magazine. Full reserves and melee weapons consume nothing. Collection requires an active session, the correct floor, range and a clear line to a target just above the case handle. That target allows access from clear sides without the case blocking its own interaction. Boxes remain visible with their colliders in every zone; zone state gates interaction only. `Weapons.findNearestPickup` arbitrates drops and boxes so one E press collects one object. A full campaign reset restores every box; an ordinary retry restores the complete saved ledger together with the exact weapon inventory.

Ammo-box positions are floor origins, not mesh centres. Each low olive case rests on two rails, has a lid, handle and latches, and labels its lid and front with AMMO. Static parts are instanced by shared geometry/material for five meshes per box and no added lights. Register each box as bearing on its actual deck, and validate the gallery lane, rooftop route, authored enemy spawn pockets and declared approach point against its full visible collider bounds when adding or moving supplies.

Normal checkpoint retries preserve one-way gates behind the player. A full development reset calls `Triggers.reset()` first: trigger flags clear and authored reset hooks disable their cached fire, smoke, debris and colliders. Reentry reuses those objects. `Colliders.setEnabled` only accepts registered boxes, and an inactive fire keeps its light source at zero intensity rather than allocating a new light on every replay.

The street choice commits by entering an arena at street height. Committing a branch stops ordinary arrivals, keeps living contacts and saves a checkpoint for that branch. Crossing another trigger afterward must not cancel its enemies, timer, or objective. Gnucci's car occupies the east bay at `x=32, z=23`; its shared `commitRadius` leaves the straight scaffold exit and bakery crossing clear. The car finale has eight bodyguards in two groups and also requires reaching the car after they are defeated. The bakery finale uses its eighteen-contact roster and a 180-second deadline. Both wait for all authored groups, including pending and unstarted contacts; clearing only the opening group cannot win. The bakery deadline advances on simulation time and therefore stops during pause.

## Enemy navigation and perception

`EnemyNavigationPlanner` builds spatial buckets from the active collision list and lazily caches walkable cells and edges on floor layers. It uses a 0.7 m grid and incremental A* with a shared limit of 96 expansions per slice, no more often than every 1/30 second of simulation time. A search stops after 4,096 expansions; cached layers and replan frequency are also bounded. These are work limits, not a frame-rate guarantee. Short support/clearance probes handle ordinary steps; detours are requested when a direct approach is blocked.

Changing `Colliders.revision` invalidates cached geometry and routes. Disabling a one-way gate must therefore use the collider API, not mutate the list behind the planner. Removing or recycling an enemy cancels its pending route as well as its attack. Paths own a copy of the observed goal. Investigation can allow more time for a detour, up to twelve seconds, but it cannot reveal the player's new unseen position or refresh its own observation by replanning.

Stair pursuers additionally follow the shared `STAIRS` route, split at the actual flight ends. Their waypoints cross between lanes only on the end landings and lead through the real tower doorways. Elevation participates in route projection, so a target on the floor above does not become a target directly underneath it. Melee enemies on stairs keep climbing until within attack height. The same bounded observation memory applies; waypoints never query an unseen player's new position. Ordinary spawns retain their previous attack timing, while primed rear arrivals alone receive the grace period.

This is a bounded grid planner, not a navmesh, a general multi-storey navigation system or a full crowd solver. Stacked floors, curbs, furniture corners, sightlines and movement integration still need actual actor tests and silent play after layout changes. Unit coverage of the planner alone cannot establish that an NPC reaches a target in the full scene.

## Player contact and pickup rules

`spawnFire` registers world-space damage bounds and a flame-center source for every fire, including effects without movement collision. Blocking fires use their collision extent; unblocked fires use their visual width and height, excluding smoke and light range. `createFireHazards` tests the actual player capsule with a 2 cm contact tolerance after movement on the fixed simulation clock. Solid colliders shield contact, excluding fire barriers themselves. Active contact applies 20 HP/s once across overlapping patches; leaving stops damage immediately. The mission damage path bypasses armor for fire, throttles blood feedback to 0.25 s, and creates no enemy-direction warning. Death and retry reset contact state; lethal contact ends that gameplay step before pickups or checkpoint/ending commits. Pause never advances damage, and inactive cached gates remain harmless until their trigger reactivates them.

`WEAPON_DEFS` supplies melee duration and contact phase as well as damage, range and cooldown. Fists contact at 140 ms, the bat at 250 ms, and the knife at 130 ms. `beginMelee` records an owner/type and starts the windup without storing an enemy. At the single contact event, `Weapons` queries the current camera direction, reach and world occlusion, choosing at most one nearest target from a narrow ray fan. A target leaving reach or moving behind cover during windup cannot receive a cached hit. Pause stops this simulation timeline; equip, drop, restore, death and a valid firearm reload cancel it.

A successful contact briefly holds only the held pose, not the simulation or input. Essential melee travel remains visible under reduced motion; movement bob is removed. Damage, cooldown, range and ammunition tuning stay separate from pose code. New melee animations must peak at the authored contact phase rather than applying damage on button press.

V (RB/R1 or R3) uses fists while carrying a firearm, preserving the inventory slot and all ammunition. The cached fist view model temporarily replaces the gun for the attack, then restores the same firearm and its muzzle anchor. Primary fire cannot overlap a punch; reload blocks or cancels the melee timeline. The gun remains the owner of the temporary attack so a weapon change invalidates contact normally.

`RAGE_CONFIG` defines the rage health threshold, minimum kills, rolling window, boost multiplier and deadline. The defaults require living health strictly below 30% and four credited kills within 60 gameplay seconds. T or D-pad up doubles current HP, leaving the 100-HP maximum unchanged. A credited kill before the 10-second deadline secures the remaining health without another heal. Otherwise, the original activation HP is restored, even after intervening damage or healing; death cancels the wager without resurrection. Main advances rage before attacks so a kill at the deadline is too late. The same simulation gate freezes both timers during pause. Death, checkpoint retry and combat-stat reset clear the history and pending outcome; ordinary zone changes do not. HUD presentation does not own the timers.

`ThreatFeedback` checks actual windups, firing bursts and recent damaging actors against the rendered camera after weapon aim updates its FOV. `offscreen-threats.js` tests the entire body against the yaw/pitch/FOV/aspect/zoom frustum; a partly visible body is not called offscreen. Amber windup warnings and red hit warnings identify direction without audio. The newest unseen hit takes priority; otherwise the nearest unseen attack is shown, with a unique-source count. Hit history retains up to eight live actor references for 1.1 seconds of simulation, not wall time. Seeing the actor acknowledges the hit, preventing it from reappearing on a later turn away. Removed/dead sources, player death, checkpoint restore and completed play clear feedback. Pause freezes its time. The HUD owns only presentation and cached DOM writes, not threat selection or timers.

`HUD.setHealth` derives the persistent health warning from unrounded, clamped health: critical at `0 < HP < 20`, low at `20 <= HP < 40`, and off at `HP >= 40`, zero health or a visible death screen. It updates the edge tint, warning text and accessible health description independently of the transient blood-flash timer. `clearFeedback` clears transient effects without erasing health state; `showDeath` recomputes the warning even if health did not change. Damage, healing, full reset and checkpoint restoration call `setHealth`. Pause, briefing and ending overlays hide the HUD while retaining that state. The tint has no animation or transition and adds no renderer pass.

Optional armor and reserve-ammunition readouts retain layout space while hidden. Fixed health-meter geometry and reserved number, warning and status space keep the main HUD readouts steady through armor, health and weapon changes.

`placeWeaponDrop` tries a bounded set of rotations and offsets within 20 cm, computes real mesh bounds, and checks supporting floor corners and blocking solids. Random yaw is preserved when it fits; exact world axes let long weapons rest across narrow treads. Pickups lie on their side instead of balancing on a grip or clipping through the floor. An unsupported fallback reports `settled: false`; this is static placement, not a falling rigid-body simulation.

## Projectile surfaces and movement

Movement uses `Colliders` and its authored capsule-clearance boxes. Shots and enemy sight use `Ballistics`, built from the final visible geometry after batching and horizontal surface ownership. Do not enlarge a chair's collision box to make its back stop bullets: the chair's actual seat, back and legs already participate, while their open gaps stay open. The stair guards and protective screens retain their movement safety volumes without turning those volumes into bulletproof panels.

The index builds world-space bounds for meshes and instances, then shared per-geometry triangle trees. Nearest hits include point, normal, material and `surfaceKind`; callers can provide reusable hit records. Player fire compares the nearest actor against the world hit, and separately checks camera-to-barrel and barrel-to-impact paths. NPCs check their shoulder-to-barrel path and use the same world query. A view model drawn through nearby furniture therefore cannot start a shot beyond it. Melee checks current geometry at contact and can produce a surface impact without awarding a hit or kill.

`bullet` queries include solid glass; `sight` queries pass transparent glazing. Alpha-cutout screens use cached texture pixels so their holes remain open. Missing or unreadable masks remain open and are counted in diagnostics. This is hitscan with geometric occlusion, not material penetration, ricochet, simulated bullets or destructible glass. Enemy damage still uses the established head/body hit volumes.

Build the index before pooled NPC rigs and dropped weapons enter `World`. Render-only effects opt out through `userData.ballistics = false`. Register new physical solids with `Ballistics.addObject`; the neighbor's one-way debris does this once and links its collider so enable/visibility changes remain live. Transform, material or mask changes require `updateObject`; geometry-buffer changes require a rebuild. Nothing traverses the whole scene or reads texture pixels on each shot. These rules are separate from nav-grid invalidation through `Colliders.revision`.

## Audio and silence

`Audio` owns every audio device, source and sample. Gameplay supplies events and context only. `main.js` applies `audioMixFromSettings` on preference changes and sends accepted simulation time, zone, nearby-contact pressure and the camera listener to `Audio.tick`. The score is an original low-register harmonic bed and pulse driven by `audio-score.js`; it does not advance or accumulate missed notes while paused. Continuous ambience, action effects, score and radio have separate buses under Master. Radio ducks Music without changing the stored user preference.

`street-atmosphere.js` schedules intermittent positional alarm pulses and a distant siren through the Ambience bus. Cues leave quiet gaps, defer during combat or radio, and attenuate as the listener moves; active cues also duck behind those foreground sounds. The bakery lowers their gain and filtering cutoff. Accepted simulation time, pause, zone exits and mute control their lifecycle, so hidden or muted play cannot queue a burst for later. Offline graph and scheduling tests check this behavior; hard-muted browser QA does not assess audible quality.

Footsteps read the material under the actual player's feet when a contact occurs; crouching, sprinting and vertical landing force affect their weight. Reload sounds follow accepted start, insertion at 66% progress, and completion events. They cannot finish a cancelled weapon's reload through an independent timer. Melee distinguishes a swing from an actual body or world contact. Positional gunshots and impacts carry their source point and environment. Shotgun pellets retain their individual damage/visual contacts while producing at most one world and one body impact sound per trigger pull.

`checkpoint-comms.js` maps eight short licensed recordings to checkpoint IDs and matching radio subtitles. Original story captions remain distinct; the recordings do not pretend to speak that longer dialogue. Navigation retains only the current cue during a bounded audio-context resume wait. The audio controller can then wait briefly for its local sample using simulation time, never start playback from a fetch completion. Expired, superseded, muted, paused and reset cues cannot replay later. An optional speech fallback uses only voices explicitly marked local by the browser.

The provenance manifest is `public/assets/audio/manifest.json`; the bundle imports the smaller generated `core/audio-catalog.json`. Run `node tools/update-audio-catalog.mjs` after changing the manifest. The asset tests require an exact match, local WAV paths, source/license records, hashes, short mono PCM16 data, output headroom and silent endpoints. This technical validation does not establish perceived sound quality. Audio credits disclose the generic human performances, object/toy mechanical substitutes and remaining source limitations.

The engine caps active voices at 64, noise buffers at four, concurrent sample workers at two, and decoded samples at 32 / 16 MiB. Warmup does not repeatedly reload evicted files. Audio tests inject in-memory graphs, decoders and speech adapters; they never use a real device. The permanent policy in `audio-policy.js` still locks `mute=1`, `qa=1` and WebDriver sessions off. Those sessions cannot allocate a context, nodes, decoded buffers, speech, or even request audio samples. Mix and voice preferences never grant playback permission. Normal sessions also start muted and require an explicit user unmute; pause, mute, death and restart cancel playback and pending work.

## Rendering and resource budgets

`createLightBudget` snapshots authored point lights after world, environment, effects, weapons, and pickup lights exist. It exposes a constant pool of **eight point lights** to the renderer, choosing relevant sources by active zone, distance, and intensity. Authored lights act as data sources; their visibility is disabled during selection. Ambient/hemisphere light and the single shadow-casting directional light are separate from this budget.

Create new practical-light sources before the budget is constructed, or pass them to the idempotent `lightBudget.register(light)` method. Progression fire gates register their sources before rendering. Adding arbitrary point lights during play can bypass the budget and change shader variants. Animate/reuse existing sources instead. `fitWorldShadow` supplies the conservative world fit for the single 2048 × 2048 directional shadow map. `createFocusedShadowBudget` can crop it to half, three-quarter or full width/height when the complete potentially shadowed visible receiver volume fits. Whole-texel shifts and hysteresis stabilize the sampling grid; wide views retain the full fit. The caster envelope is gathered once, while updates clip fixed volume edges without traversing meshes or increasing map size. Performance quality disables shadows; automatic quality separately adjusts render resolution. These controls do not promise a frame rate on every GPU.

`createRoofTaskLighting` is constructed before that scan. It moves the uniquely matched existing service-house lamp beside the doors and adds a sheltered exit bulkhead below the parapet coping. Both use the existing light-selection pool; no extra shadow map or screen pass is added. `setEnabled` restores the original service fixture/source state and returns moved meshes for QA to refresh in `Ballistics`; disabling the exit source sets its intensity to zero. Disposal restores originals and releases only owned hardware resources. The helper adds no frame-loop work.

Exterior detail uses the environment's shared batches for masonry heads, a service-house louver and conduit, drainage hardware and supported roof cowls. It adds no movement barriers or lights. After surface ownership, only named roof decks receive a cached material clone with a shared low-frequency membrane map, while the existing close-range tar maps remain. This adds a texture sample to those decks, not another geometry or full-screen pass, and leaves shared non-roof materials unchanged.

`applyWaterTankStaveUV` changes only the main rooftop cylinder's side UVs so the existing wood maps read as vertical staves. Caps, positions, indices, normals, supports and material remain unchanged; it adds no geometry, draws, textures or frame-loop work. The selected strip deliberately stretches longitudinal grain to avoid board end joints, so it is not a measured wood-grain scale.

The objective sedan retains `createSedanCabin` for closed tapered glazing, fitted pillars and a crowned roof, plus the shaped hood and rolled bumpers from `sedan-panels.js`; `DISTRICT.car` places it in the east bay. `createCivilianVehicle` caches five silhouettes independently of their paint/finish and shares hardware, glazing, tire and lamp materials. Each instance has six meshes, no texture maps or lights, and fresh transform/bounds objects. `DISTRICT.street.parkedCars` owns fixed IDs, variants, finishes and uneven parking angles. `placeCivilianVehicle` measures transformed tire vertices to set support height, including the hatchback's longitudinal roll between the 0.05 m road and 0.14 m pavement, then transforms the lower-body and cabin envelopes into two registered movement boxes. Glass, mirrors and the complete upper body contribute to the upper envelope; bullets still follow final visible triangles, including open underbody gaps. Panel-van cargo is opaque cover, while the passenger van has actual glass openings with fitted pillars. New construction occurs only during world startup. Development QA also precaches all five designs for paused inspection, hiding one authored vehicle while displaying a render-only substitute; every reset or return to gameplay removes the preview and restores the original. Production only constructs installed variants. See [vehicle provenance](civilian-vehicle-provenance.md), [car review](civilian-vehicle-review.md) and [van review](civilian-van-review.md).

`buildStreetVehicleAftermath` darkens a cloned paint finish on the far civilian sedan without moving its vertices, derives the engine fire from its actual transform, and adds curved skid marks that break at curb height changes. The existing fire/smoke system supplies the localized effect and light; its contact-damage bounds are separate from the car's movement body. `buildStreetAftermath` batches a market spill near `x=-3.5, z=27.7` and broken deli glass with a fallen sign near `x=-13.5, z=27.7`. The tipped crate has matching solid collision; smaller debris stays shallow enough to step over. These additions precede ballistic indexing and preserve the bakery route and enemy arrival space.

`refineConcreteBarrier` replaces only the central street cover box with a closed Jersey profile: 28 triangles instead of 12, a 16-triangle increase with no added draw, material, texture or light. The two existing reflectors fit the sloped face. Its conservative movement box is unchanged; ballistic queries follow the actual tapered surface and its open upper corners.

`createInteriorLighting` builds one 512² linear RGBA atlas after surface ownership and before ballistic indexing. Selected static faces in the apartments and bakery receive low-frequency fixture illumination and diffuse ambient visibility; exact static triangles gate rays. The bake caps work at 300,000 rays, reduces chart density to fit, and yields during startup. The atlas is 1 MiB without mipmaps, with separate UV attributes on cloned geometry. Live lights still supply moving actors, fire, flicker and specular response. This authored diffuse fill is not a GI/radiosity solver. It adds no draws or lights and performs no bake work during play.

`createInteriorReflections` captures the apartment kitchen, neighbor kitchen and bakery preparation room once, using 64² cube faces and PMREM filtering. The three retained maps occupy about 1.97 MiB; temporary cube/shadow resources are released. A separate static capture scene excludes actors, pickups, fire and changing gates. Room-bound assignments add no render draws or per-frame captures, but still require environment-map sampling. The sky environment is the unsupported/failure fallback. These are static local reflections, not mirrors or reflections of moving enemies.

Enemy rigs are prebuilt per archetype and reused. Capacity sums each campaign encounter's survivor allowance, then adds enough slots for one chosen finale and a corpse reserve. Allowances respect finite rosters, live/type caps and rear-arrival melee substitutions; the bakery's shared finale roster is counted once. Living actors retain their pool ownership across checkpoints and branch selection. Releasing a rig invalidates its old owner before another enemy can acquire it. Corpses expire and are capped; they must not hold pool slots forever or keep a delayed attack alive. Shared character surfaces and car/building primitives avoid repeated geometry construction. `pushDecor` and `flushDecor` merge static decoration per material, while `environment.js` instances repeated surroundings. Static solid props intentionally contribute their visible geometry to projectile queries, including batched and instanced parts. Movement collision remains separately authored; visual-only fire, smoke and effects must not become solid cover.

NPC rendering combines GPU-skinned garment/skin meshes with rigid head/detail meshes. Connected authored surfaces retain the animation skeleton and gameplay anchors. The neck blends chest, neck and head influences beneath a shaped jaw; ears grow from the head surface. Folded collars, sleeve hems, sewn pockets and role-specific vest panels merge into the garment geometry. The former primitive meshes remain hidden `bounds-proxy` objects and must not be counted as visible anatomy or warmed as body assets. Conservative bone-influence boxes support cheap bounds work; `getHumanoidVisualBounds` scans the actual posed visible vertices only for explicit inspection.

The brawler's shoulder and short-sleeve silhouette is shaped once in the existing bind frame, retaining topology and draw counts without extra animation-loop work. The prior collar is retained; the experimental neck-sampled collar fit is not part of the accepted model.

`enemies.js` measures completed horizontal displacement after `EnemyNavigation.moveBody`, derives speed from distance/time, and projects actual travel onto the actor's forward/strafe axes. The rig eases stride transitions and feeds a flat support phase and lifted return phase into its existing leg IK; a melee attack immediately stops locomotion stride. This prevents intended movement against an obstacle from driving a full walk cycle. It is authored movement-driven gait, not terrain-aware foot locking or a guarantee of zero sliding during turns and speed changes.

Deaths retain the 0.52-second authored collapse. A bounded support-point cloud drawn from the actual skinned surfaces supplies contact bounds during collapse; settled bodies reuse their final offset. This preserves floor/gallery contact without a full vertex scan every frame. The cloud is an approximation, so visual-fit QA must still inspect actual surface vertices. This is not a physical ragdoll system. Pool reset clears collapse and attack state before reuse.

The world renders on layer 0, then `renderWithViewModel` clears only depth and draws camera-held meshes on layer 1. Its optional fourth argument supplies the world render callback. Weapon materials retain depth tests and writes, so parts hide one another without clipping through nearby world geometry. Lights are shared with both layers. The weapon pass does not repaint the sky or regenerate shadows; renderer statistics include both passes, and temporary render state is restored even if rendering fails.

`main.js` wires this callback to `createWorldPresentation`. On auto/high quality with supported floating-point render targets, one world geometry pass supplies color and depth. GTAO reconstructs normals from that depth instead of drawing a separate normal scene, computes contact shading at half the drawing-buffer width and height, and denoises/composites it before `OutputPass` applies the final tone/color conversion. Auto uses eight AO samples; high uses twelve. The first-person layer is drawn afterward and does not receive world AO.

The world beauty target uses up to two MSAA samples in Automatic and four in High, clamped to the reported hardware limit. Switching sample count releases and rebuilds its attachments. Stable frames reuse allocations; adaptive resizes reuse presentation objects but reallocate their GPU attachments. Resolution increments land on exact hundredths so floating-point roundoff cannot disable AO prematurely at the 1.0 threshold.

High detail permits up to 2.0× render scale on smaller high-DPI viewports. Its extra allowance above the former 1.6× ceiling is limited by a 4 Mi-pixel drawing buffer; larger viewports keep the previous ceiling, and device pixel ratio remains an upper bound. This is not a universal pixel cap: an existing large 1.6× viewport can exceed that size. Automatic adaptation and the Performance scale remain unchanged.

Before enabling BEGIN MISSION or starting the animation loop, `warmCharacters` selects the actual pooled visible surfaces and weapons, compiles both direct-canvas and linear-target variants, uploads shared resources and initializes every actor's bone texture. Representative geometry/material variants are drawn in bounded startup batches; hidden legacy bounds proxies are excluded. Temporary targets are disposed and poses, hierarchy, visibility, matrices and renderer state are restored.

Boot also populates all seven held-model cache entries and calls `warmViewModels` against the actual lighting and canvas color pipeline. Unique textures and geometry upload behind the opaque loading menu, followed by a final world redraw. Warmup failures warn and fall back to ordinary first-use compilation. This moves known resource preparation into loading; it does not guarantee stall-free driver/context behavior.

Development QA optionally brackets its explicit render calls with `core/gpu-frame-timer.js`. It polls completed GPU queries without waiting, uses a bounded pool, discards invalid/disjoint results, and resets before each measured interval. No timer is imported by the production build. Benchmarks distinguish rAF cadence, CPU submission, GPU elapsed time and main-thread long tasks; none is presented as input-to-photon latency or INP.

Performance quality bypasses the world postprocessing path and releases its presentation targets; the static diffuse atlas and room reflections remain available. Unsupported floating-point targets also use the direct world render. Auto bypasses AO when adaptive pixel ratio drops below one, keeping existing resources to avoid repeated allocation at that boundary. The module reports its current state through `snapshot()` and restores renderer state after rendering. Added full-screen passes still have a GPU cost; their existence does not establish better performance or visual quality.

Production prefers the reviewed KTX2 files only on approved ASTC 4×4 or BC7/BPTC targets. ETC/BC1-only devices, unsupported formats, unavailable decoders and failed compressed triplets use the unchanged raw JPEG/PNG PBR sets. If raw loading also fails, generated albedo with derived 512² normal/roughness is tried before retaining the procedural material. `pbr-materials.js` validates all three channels and their mip/format contracts before atomically updating the shared material and physical scale; partial failures never mix sources. Detached resources are disposed without replacing the shared material object. This completes before world UVs and material clones are built.

`surface-detail.js` still builds static coordinated maps for concrete, wood, metal, asphalt, tar, roof metal, stone, rubber, gravel and tile. Furniture, hands and weapons use their own bounded shared finishes. No material data is regenerated per frame. The six accepted KTX2 maps total 5,929,019 bytes; with the 584,862-byte decoder, delivery is 6,513,881 bytes versus 9,084,707 for raw maps. Reviewed ASTC mip payload is about 8 MiB versus a 32 MiB raw RGBA8 estimate. BC7 passed offline decoded-quality and mip validation, but its hardware performance is untested. Development `?qa=1&mute=1&surfaces=raw` forces the raw pack for A/B; production ignores that override. The [compressed manifest](../public/assets/materials-ktx2-trial/manifest.json) retains the original trial filenames and exact accepted bytes, alongside the unchanged [raw source manifest](../public/assets/materials/manifest.json).

Every `MATS` entry declares `userData.surfaceKind` and `surfaceMeters`. `applyBoxWorldUV` uses that scale in both `addBox` and batched `pushDecor` unit boxes after their transforms, with texture repeat fixed at one across coordinated channels. Moving a material to a new primitive must preserve or deliberately author its UVs; the box helper does not automatically fix every cylinder, rig or instanced mesh. Environment and combat systems must not depend on remote media or runtime image generation. See the art-direction guide for provenance and the legacy generated-brick fallback's repeat limitation; see the [extended graphics review](graphics-ceiling-review.md) for current visual/performance evidence.

## Add a zone

1. Define the space in the appropriate shared layout, then add a named builder in `world/zones/` using construction helpers and `MATS`. Register actual walkable surfaces and obstacles with `Colliders`; keep purely decorative work non-colliding. Structural IDs should declare real supporting elements, and furniture should leave a clear route around it.
2. Register the builder through `buildZone` in `buildWorld`, so its world children and lights receive zone ownership. Define its trigger and objective, and update adjacent-light visibility for real sightlines. The full world is built at boot; this is not an on-demand loader.
3. Add its position in `ZONE_ORDER`, a safe foot anchor in `CHECKPOINTS`, and finite waves/spawn candidates in `ZONE_WAVE_CONFIG`. Author stage gates, live/type caps and any deliberate reinforcement or departure policy. If it belongs to a new ending, update branch validation and explicit reset/restore paths as well.
4. Add navigation targets and original captions in `game/navigation.js`. Stair or drop targets must account for foot height, not just horizontal proximity. Health packs are declared in `game/health-supply-data.js` and initialized once by mission; floor ammo boxes are declared in `ammo-supply-rules.js` and initialized once before the first mission checkpoint.
5. Extend mission-data, builder, collision and NPC-route tests. Check actual geometry, not only a second copy of its coordinates; navigation caches must observe collider revisions. In silent development QA, inspect checkpoint support and clearance, travel the route normally, clear or intentionally bypass staged contacts, die/restart, and check neighboring sightlines. Record observed results separately from implementation notes.

## Add a weapon

1. Add its tuning to `WEAPON_DEFS`: attack kind, damage, range and cadence. Melee weapons need `attackDuration`, `contactPhase` and `contactArc`; ranged weapons need magazine, reload, spread, pellet and recoil values. `full: true` requests held automatic fire.
2. Implement or reuse one canonical weapon asset for the view model, world pickup and enemy attachment where applicable. Keep material and geometry ownership compatible with the existing pools and test actual pickup bounds on floors and stairs.
3. Extend snapshot/reload rules only where the new behavior requires it. Preserve ammo across checkpoints, handle empty magazines and zero reserve, and make pickup/drop behavior explicit.
4. If adding a sound, route it through `Audio` with its early silent check. Do not construct an audio context or node in weapon code. Add unit coverage for new balance rules and silent lifecycle behavior, then test firing/reload/pickup/restart through a muted session.

## Add an enemy

1. Add an archetype to `ENEMY_TYPES` in `enemies.js`, separating visual proportions/colors from health, movement, perception, attack tuning and weapon drops. The current archetypes are thug, brawler, gunman, bruiser, hitman and enforcer.
2. Reuse `makeHumanoid` or deliberately extend its rig contract. `root.userData.rig` exposes joints, anchors, dimensions, visible surfaces, legacy bounds meshes, support samples, pose state and reset. Use `visualMeshes` for rendered-body work; `bodyMeshes` includes hidden proxies. Use `updateHumanoidPose` and `resetHumanoidPose`; never parent a weapon to a scaled limb mesh. Grip and muzzle anchors use world-metre dimensions, and head hit bounds follow the articulated head. Ensure `EnemyPool` can preallocate enough slots for normal waves and both final squads. Shared geometry and appearance materials must not be disposed when a single actor is released.
3. Put reusable damage, visibility-memory, attack-windup or melee-validation rules in `combat-rules.js`. A lost line of sight must not reveal a player's unseen position. Interrupted or released enemies must not finish an old attack.
4. Add the archetype to a finite authored encounter and test spawn deferral, death, corpse retirement, pool reuse and checkpoint clearing. Check its body dimensions against navigation clearance and verify the derived pool capacity under overlapping reserves and finals. Test the readable attack cue, line of sight and actual movement in silent browser play; unit rules alone cannot prove those qualities.

## Verification boundaries

`npm run check` runs lint, Node tests and a production build. The suite includes pure rule tests and geometry/actor fixtures for collision, authored rooms and stairs, navigation, encounter schedules, material data, hand poses and pooled ownership. Builder fixtures inject scene services and use Three.js math without starting a renderer or audio. These checks do not by themselves establish full browser route completion, visual quality, controller hardware compatibility or sustained performance.

For browser work, use `?qa=1&mute=1` with the development server. QA installation is gated by `import.meta.env.DEV`; production builds omit that inspection interface. The audio hard lock also applies to `?mute=1` in production and cannot be undone from the menu. Muted sound calls and resume operations do not create a Web Audio context, nodes, or sample buffers; normal audio initialization is lazy and requires explicit unmuting.

The controlled regression suite temporarily selects authored timing; individual variation fixtures use explicit fixed seeds. `EncounterSeeds.setOverride` returns the exact prior override (`undefined` for fresh attempts, `null` for authored, or a uint32). Restore that value before the final apartment reset, including on setup failure, cancellation and disposal. Installation, normal resets, inspectors and manual benchmarks must not pin ordinary gameplay to a fixture seed.

The paused NPC inspector uses actual pooled actors and production pose helpers, with type and pose selections. The held-weapon inspector changes only the visible pose of the real bat, fists or knife, with no active attack or damage. The separate balcony melee benchmark measures a disclosed controlled fixture, not a full campaign playthrough. It uses real attack/input/AI paths while replacing defeated contacts and restoring health to keep a bounded workload. Record those interventions alongside performance numbers.

The separate visible NPC motion review samples living poses through the production driver, then uses actual death, release and same-slot reacquisition paths. Its walking is in place and its attack poses apply no damage. The stair review instead runs real first-flight AI pursuit and fixed-step simulation with an independent tracking camera. Both are marked `VISUAL-ONLY`, allow pause/resume, restore the apartment and abort on hiding, resizing or disposal. Screenshot capture belongs to these visual runs, never the measured benchmarks. The report parser in `tools/art-review-report.mjs` keeps callback cadence distinct from presented FPS and leaves unreported GPU p99/maximum and CPU p99 unavailable.

**Inspect world objects** selects the actual health case, firearm/knife drops, sedan cabin, water tank or central barrier. **Full drop pool (16)** creates sixteen machine-gun drops through `WeaponDrops.spawn` and normal placement/halo selection. All these inspections pause simulation, exclude enemies and make no collection or ammunition transfer. Their specimen counts describe material groups per color pass; shadow passes can repeat draws. Use **Return to game menu** to clear fixtures before play. The [two-cycle graphics review](graphics-two-cycle-review.md) records the associated comparisons and final validation; older reports remain evidence for their own builds.

Keep actual browser findings, device details, screenshots and measurements in a separate validation record. Historical scripts in `tools/legacy/`, pre-existing untracked root scripts, and `shots/` are preserved context, not maintained browser automation. Do not reuse their raw browser-launch commands as a substitute for the supported silent workflow.
