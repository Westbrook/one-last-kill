# Art direction and reference provenance

The target is a grounded, readable urban crime thriller: worn materials, plausible spaces, restrained color, recognizable civilian surroundings, and short dangerous encounters with breathing room between them. This is an original fan-made campaign inspired by *The Punisher: One Last Kill*, not an official adaptation or a reconstruction of its film frames and dialogue. Cinematic presentation is a direction for iteration, not proof that the browser prototype has reached AAA console production quality.

## Verified reference material

Research was performed on August 27, 2026 using official Disney/Marvel material. Static publicity images were inspected without playing trailers, video, or audio. No official still, poster, film footage or screenplay was added to this repository as a game asset.

The official interview describes Frank seeking meaning after revenge, Ma Gnucci pursuing payback, a quiet psychological opening, and later action driven by character and survival. These are tone and story-context references, not specifications for this game's mission. [Official Disney interview](https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/news/the-punisher-one-last-kill/)

## Deliberate creative differences

The connected apartment/balcony/stairwell/roof/scaffolding/street/bakery route, its exact geometry, the Little Sicily mission setting, finite squads, checkpoint behavior, and player-selected car/bakery endings are this game's authored construction. Their presence does not establish that those events or routes occur in the special. The expanded service roof, open lightwell, shop fronts, bakery preparation room and interior furnishings are also original level design. Dusk lighting, the skyline composition, HUD, weapon balance, pickup halos and navigation cues are game presentation choices.

The inspected street still is daylight; the darker game palette is therefore a creative choice rather than a claim of frame accuracy. Keep practical lighting, believable materials and ordinary architecture as the common ground. Any new film-specific detail should have a primary reference, and any invented detail should remain identified as original game content.

## Visual priorities

| Area | Direction | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Materials | Dusty sage-gray plaster, umber brick, dark worn wood, scuffed metal and restrained cloth detail | Keep material scale consistent between surfaces; avoid oversized stains, painted-in directional shadows, or plastic specular highlights. |
| Light | Warm utility lamps against cooler exterior light, with visible route and enemy silhouettes | Darkness must not hide stairs, exits, pickup prompts or attack cues. Use the existing eight-light practical budget rather than adding unbounded point lights. |
| Space | Door frames, conduits, lintels, rooftop hardware, distant windows, scattered mundane belongings | Preserve traversal clearance and distinguish decorative background objects from real cover. |
| Atmosphere | Layered distant buildings and moderate haze, small particles and localized effects | Keep contrast around targets; avoid filling the screen with smoke, glare or unrelated neon color. |
| Characters | Distinct proportions, clothing, weapons and readable movement/attack preparation | Different enemy roles should remain recognizable without relying only on an outline or label. Current procedural rigs are a prototype limitation. |
| Combat | Responsive input, legible impacts, useful cover, ammunition decisions and recovery beats | Effects should confirm an action rather than obscure it. Reduced-motion settings must remain usable. |
| Story | Original captions, environmental evidence of civilian life, and a meaningful protection choice | The mission must remain understandable with sound permanently off. Do not make a spoken line the sole source of a necessary instruction. |
| Interface | Restrained typography, clear objectives, consistent ammunition/health feedback | The persistent low-health edge tint stays static and leaves the centre readable; decorative styling must not disguise menu focus or interaction states. |

## Spaces and encounter rhythm

The opening apartments keep their existing exterior walls and exits but use partitions and furnishings to suggest daily life. The player's sleeping alcove, kitchenette, storage and sitting area provide different views of the room. The neighbor's foyer opens around a dining table into a living area and kitchen. Refrigerators, cookers, shelves, coat hooks and supported cabinets should read as useful objects, not scattered cover boxes. Varied books, small framed landscapes, refrigerator notes, patterned rugs, folded throws and kitchen boards/bowls/tins supply everyday context. They rest on their supporting surfaces and use shared opaque finishes. Keep the starting sidestep pocket and the routes through these rooms clear; dense decoration is not a reason to force extra jumps.

Outside, the 1.8 m gallery remains a close encounter space. The stair tower has four flights, each with fourteen treads over a 4.2 m run and deep turning landings, so the route reads as a connected circulation space. The roof then opens into a service yard: a mechanical house, ventilation equipment, water tank, maintenance supplies, drainage and guarded edges explain both the building's function and the combat cover. Its 935 m² plan area excludes the open lightwell but still includes equipment and parapets; it is not all free walking space. The lightwell preserves a real exterior opening for the first apartment's north window.

Four offset scaffold decks lead down into a larger district with pavement, parked vehicles, separate shop fronts and street cover. The bakery contains a public shop and a connected preparation room with an oven and work surfaces. Scored loaves, varied paper packages and a board/rolling-pin/bowl arrangement make the shop's purpose readable; the preparation island uses its own steel finish instead of the generic weathered outdoor metal. Shared `ROOF`, `STAIRS` and `DISTRICT` definitions keep these places, their entrances and encounter positions aligned. Background shops remain scenery unless an interior is explicitly built.

The player begins with fists. The first flat has two melee contacts; the neighbor adds the first armed opponent after its opening pair. The finite zone rosters rise through 2, 4, 8, 8, 12, 14, 16 and 18 contacts, with smaller limits on how many can be alive at once. This progression creates room for weapon acquisition and recovery instead of immediately filling every room with gunfire.

The roof's first two sentries must be defeated before finite reserves begin arriving; later groups can overlap, with at most five contacts and one enforcer alive. Passing a stair landing or descending beyond a scaffold deck can leave its contacts behind without granting kills or clear rewards. At street level, the car finale has eight bodyguards, while protecting the bakery means facing its eighteen-contact roster within 180 seconds of active simulation. These are authored pacing choices, not evidence that the experience is balanced, difficult or compelling for every player. The entire mission must remain understandable without sound.

## Assets and source provenance

Visual assets combine original code-authored surfaces, generated images and licensed CC0 material maps. Runtime files are served locally from `public/assets/`; no film still or actor photograph is used as a game texture. Source records distinguish these methods rather than describing the whole asset library as generated.

| Asset | Current use and origin | Source record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `last-kill-keyart.png` | Generated menu image: a rear-facing fictional vigilante and tenement doorway | `public/assets/last-kill-keyart.prompt.txt` |
| `materials/red_brick/` | Raw source/fallback for the 1024² brick diffuse, OpenGL normal and roughness set; 1.4 m repeat | [Poly Haven Red Brick](https://polyhaven.com/a/red_brick), [raw manifest](../public/assets/materials/manifest.json) |
| `materials/plastered_wall_03/` | Raw source/fallback for the 1024² plaster set; 4 m repeat, with the game's sage tint | [Poly Haven Plastered Wall 03](https://polyhaven.com/a/plastered_wall_03), same raw manifest |
| `materials-ktx2-trial/` | Accepted compressed delivery of both sets on ASTC 4×4 or BC7/BPTC; trial filenames retain the exact reviewed bytes | [Compressed manifest](../public/assets/materials-ktx2-trial/manifest.json) |
| `plaster-aged.png`, `brick-weathered.png` | Earlier generated albedo, retained as complete load-failure fallbacks | `public/assets/textures.prompt.txt` |
| `characters/face-albedo-trial.png` | Accepted generated fictional adult face color, projected onto the authored head; filename retains its trial suffix | `public/assets/characters/face-albedo-trial.prompt.txt` |

The two default wall sets are by Rob Tuytel and provided under [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license). The local manifest retains source URLs, authors, channel conventions, physical spans, sizes and hashes. Color is sampled as sRGB; normal and roughness are linear data, with no green-channel inversion for the OpenGL normals. All channels share the same UV transform. Raw sources and their provenance remain unchanged; source PNG bit depth is not a claim about GPU precision. Production now defaults to the accepted KTX2 encodings on approved ASTC 4×4 or BC7/BPTC targets. The six maps total 5,929,019 bytes plus a 584,862-byte decoder (6,513,881 bytes combined), versus 9,084,707 bytes for raw maps. Reviewed ASTC mip payload is about 8 MiB versus the 32 MiB raw RGBA8 estimate; these are payload estimates, not measured driver residency. All six maps passed ASTC visual review and offline BC7 quality/mip checks. BC7 hardware performance remains untested.

`loadSurfaceTextures` commits a complete PBR triplet and physical scale before world geometry or material clones are constructed. ETC/BC1-only targets, unsupported formats or decoder/compressed-load failures fall back to the raw PBR triplets. Partial failures release candidate maps; a raw PBR failure then tries the earlier generated albedo with coordinated normal/roughness derived from a 512² sample. If that also fails, procedural materials remain intact. Those derived channels are authored estimates, not independently measured PBR data. The older generated brick has a partial-course/mortar band at its vertical boundary; that known fallback limitation does not describe the new default Red Brick set. Later generated brick trials were rejected, with their prompt history retained. Development QA can force the raw reference using `?qa=1&mute=1&surfaces=raw`; production ignores this URL override.

The face source was made with the built-in image generation tool without an actor reference. The tool did not expose an exact model selector, so its provenance does not claim GPT Image 2. The unchanged 1254² image retains mild directional shading and eye glints despite the requested flat albedo. The accepted material therefore uses a masked, palette-relative diffuse blend on authored head UVs; eyes, brows, hair and side/back regions retain their authored treatment. It is not a scan, a facial-animation system or independent normal/roughness data. One shared texture uses about 8 MiB with mipmaps, adds no body draw, and falls back to authored skin if loading fails.

Other surfaces remain code-authored. `surface-detail.js` supplies coordinated color, shallow normal relief and roughness for concrete, wood boards, metal, asphalt, tar, roof metal, stone, rubber, gravel and tile. Furniture adds sealed wood grain, quiet linen/tinted upholstery, appliance enamel, hardware and opaque oven glazing. Shared story atlases supply books, paper details and rugs; bakery provisions add crust/crumb and package finishes. Roof decks use a separate shared membrane variation over the existing close-range tar maps. Hands and weapons share their own bounded finishes. These maps are built once and reused, with no per-frame texture generation.

`SURFACE_METERS` and material metadata define physical repeats. `world-uv.js` applies them to structural and batched boxes, while custom furniture, hands and weapon profiles author suitable UVs separately. Keep all PBR channels aligned and test both close views and repeated architecture. Retain the exact prompt or source/license record, preparation steps and known limitations whenever replacing an asset.

## Character, furniture and lighting presentation

NPC bodies use connected authored garment/skin surfaces with GPU skinning. Shaped jaw/neck transitions, integrated ear surfaces, folded collars, sleeve hems, pockets and role-specific vest panels strengthen their silhouettes and clothing construction. The existing skeleton still controls weight transfer, grips and attack cues. Stride follows completed movement after collision, with eased stance/swing transitions; it is not terrain-aware foot locking. Hidden legacy bounds meshes are not visible anatomy. Explicit visual-fit checks scan the actual posed surfaces; a bounded support-point cloud helps the authored collapse stay against floors and gallery limits without scanning every rendered vertex each frame. Deaths remain authored pose transitions, not ragdolls.

The brawler's shoulders and short sleeves are shaped within the existing topology, retaining the prior collar. Review front, profile and moving attack views; a smooth portrait alone does not establish a sound animated fit.

The coordinated character finish uses one restrained skin palette across the face, jaw, neck and arms, with regional roughness for skin, eyes and hair. Continuous hair perimeters remove the former abrupt temple step. Garment UVs, directional yarns and localized edge wear distinguish jersey, woven cloth, leather, rubber and armor within the existing body draws. Two immutable bytes per vertex carry roughness and microdetail weight; the existing shared maps remain local and are generated only at startup. The generated face source is unchanged and retains its documented limitations. See [character finish provenance](character-finish-provenance.md).

The seven closed shops share a small kit of timber/enamel fronts, offset or central doors, split shutters, tall sash, paired casement and industrial windows. The pharmacy's cream fascia, laundry's sage paint, barber's oxblood board and quieter deli/market/trade signs break the repeated street rhythm while the bakery retains its warm open objective. Blinds, shelves and washer/chair silhouettes remain shallow occupancy hints backed by solid shop masses. A single cached sign atlas and shared PBR maps keep the added construction bounded. See [storefront provenance](storefront-kit-provenance.md) and the [matched review](cohesive-art-review.md).

First-person hands use continuous lofted palms, joined finger roots and thumb webs, varied knuckles, flatter finger pads and shaped sleeves. Stitched glove panels and woven cuffs provide surface detail without painting directional highlights into the skin. A GPU clench morph uses immutable shared buffers; jabs still follow combat timing and reduced-motion settings. Detailed pistol, shotgun, SMG, machine-gun and knife profiles add mechanical openings, shaped stocks, blade edges and fitted hands; static parts merge by material. The bat retains its shared canonical asset. These are original game models, not actor scans or a promise that distant pickups have the same detail as held assets.

NPC firearms use reduced profiles with shaped receivers and stocks, recessed bolts, hollow barrels and open trigger guards. Shared cached geometry and two finish groups keep those details bounded while preserving the existing firing and support-hand positions. Review the silhouette in actual carry and attack poses, not only in isolated asset views.

Floor firearm drops now share those same reduced surfaces, with darker steel confined to the drop material and existing maps reused. The knife uses a separate cached blade/handle model without hands. Health supplies use a shaped medical case with a carry handle, latches and crosses, built from shared untextured finishes. Keep their original pickup cues and gameplay rules; object detail must not change the strict below-40/below-20 health warning.

Soft sofa construction, attached piping, profiled furniture legs, connected table aprons, appliance panels and restrained belongings make the apartments more legible as homes. The television has a tapered matte case, recessed vents, fitted bezel and dials, while retaining its glass, glow and grounded console support. The pass preserves all 74 apartment movement AABBs and 43 furniture support records. Cushions, the pillow/mattress, chair feet and small props are checked against actual supporting geometry; open chair shooting gaps remain open. No additional dynamic lights or alpha-heavy decoration are required.

Warm practical lights remain constrained to the eight-light pool. A bounded startup bake supplies low-frequency interior diffuse fill and ambient visibility from static triangle tests, using one 512² atlas without mipmaps. The atlas itself is 1 MiB; receiving geometry clones and extra UVs have separate storage costs. It is authored fixture lighting, not a GI/radiosity solve, and live lights still provide moving-character response, fire, flicker and specular highlights.

The roof gains fitted masonry heads, drainage, a mechanical-house louver/conduit and supported vent cowls, using shared batches rather than loose floor clutter. Low-frequency membrane variation breaks up the broad deck while retaining its rough finish. A visible service lamp is positioned beside the mechanical doors, and a sheltered bulkhead lights the exit parapet. Their compact warm pools retain the cool exterior exposure and use the same eight-light selection budget, without new shadows or screen effects.

The rooftop tank now reads as vertical timber staves by remapping its existing wood texture, without adding slats or geometry. Civilian vehicles form a small family of compact hatchback, conventional sedan, longer wagon, panel van and passenger van silhouettes. Muted gray, brown and olive paint, rubber trim and quiet lower-body wear suggest different owners. The vans share a taller cab and cargo roof, with solid loading doors or genuine passenger window openings. The panel van occupies the middle near-curb slot; placing a taller body on the far curb would obscure the bakery doorway from the street checkpoint. Fixed parking gaps and curb setbacks vary modestly; retain road alignment, the bakery crossing and enemy arrivals. The black objective sedan keeps its longer profile, brighter idling lamps, exhaust and established location. New vehicle shapes need honest movement bounds and visible projectile contacts; a background vehicle still affects cover. See [vehicle provenance](civilian-vehicle-provenance.md), [car review](civilian-vehicle-review.md) and [van review](civilian-van-review.md). The central Jersey barrier has a broad supported toe, tapered shoulders and reflectors fitted to its face; the existing movement envelope remains conservative while bullets follow the real surface.

Three room reflections are captured once at startup from 64² cube faces in the apartment kitchen, neighbor kitchen and bakery preparation room. Their filtered maps retain about 1.97 MiB and add no recurring captures or draw calls. They reflect static surroundings, not moving enemies or changing fire gates. The single 2048² directional shadow map uses conservative half/three-quarter/full crop tiers, whole-texel shifts and hysteresis to improve useful detail without growing the map. Performance mode keeps the static bake/reflections but disables gameplay shadows and contact shading.

Auto/high GTAO reconstructs normals from world depth, works at half the drawing-buffer dimensions with eight/twelve samples, and composites before the separate weapon layer. Automatic can bypass it when adaptive resolution falls below its threshold. World edge MSAA is capped at two samples in Auto and four in High, subject to hardware support. Character and weapon warmup prepares known shader, texture, geometry and bone resources behind the loading menu. These features have startup and GPU costs; their presence alone does not prove visual quality or steady frame delivery.

The health warning is a steady edge tint while alive below 40 HP, stronger below 20 HP; the thresholds use actual health rather than the rounded displayed number. It does not pulse or replace the transient hit flash. Healing and retry recompute the warning, death suppresses it, and menu overlays hide it while preserving health state. Keep the clear centre available for aiming and threat recognition.

## Practical limits and evaluation

This renderer does not supply a film production's scans, bespoke facial animation, motion capture, large texture library, or console certification. Most geometry is procedural, static world content is created at boot, and background detail is batched or instanced. Directional lighting, bounded practical lights, shared geometry and effect pools are chosen to keep that workload manageable. NPCs use a bounded cached grid planner; it is not a navmesh or a guarantee of natural movement through every furnishing arrangement.

Evaluate changes by silently playing the route and comparing controlled views. **Inspect world objects** supplies paused object comparisons, including the full 16-drop pool; reset before playing. Check target readability, material scale, motion comfort, checkpoint recovery and frame consistency during combat. The [initial graphics review](graphics-review.md) and [extended graphics review](graphics-ceiling-review.md) preserve their historical records; the [two further cycles](graphics-two-cycle-review.md) cover the subsequent object and character refinements. Keep rAF cadence, GPU time, startup cost and actual interaction evidence separate. They are not an INP score, proof of presented FPS, or a guarantee for all hardware.
