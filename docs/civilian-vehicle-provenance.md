# Civilian vehicle art — August 31, 2026

`src/render/civilian-vehicles.js` contains original code-authored geometry for five generic neighborhood vehicles: a sedan, compact hatchback, station wagon, panel van and passenger van. The shapes do not reproduce a branded vehicle or use photographs, scans, downloaded meshes, generated bitmap images, manufacturer marks or external fonts. There are no new texture files, remote dependencies or asset downloads. The rolled bumper profile reuses the project's existing original `createSedanBumper` helper without changing that helper or the objective sedan factory.

Each body has a continuous closed stamped-metal shell with actual wheel-arch openings. Glass, front/rear pillars and the crowned roof follow each model's authored cabin profile. The hatchback and wagon have longer rear cabins than the sedan; their returned upper movement bounds include those surfaces and their mirrors. Four rounded tire profiles meet local `y=0`. Local `+X` points toward the nose. Body, glass, roof and bumper surfaces are closed, with fitted window seals, supported mirror arms and shallow door seams.

The vans use a separate construction path with a short nose, a tall continuous roof and fitted cab glazing. The panel van has opaque cargo sides, a sliding side-door seam and track, and split rear doors with supported hinges. The passenger van replaces the upper cargo panels with actual glazing openings between opaque waist panels, roof headers and posts; it adds a passenger-door treatment and step. These are static parked vehicles: doors do not open, and the dark glazing has no modeled seats or cabin controls. Both vans reuse the same wheel primitives, finishes and six material categories as the smaller cars. The three existing car variants retain identical geometry buffers, material parameters, bounds and resource counts; the van evidence includes SHA-256 comparisons of all eighteen original category buffers.

Paint and trim use shared `MeshStandardMaterial` instances. The `kept`, `used` and `workhorse` finishes vary paint roughness, restrained metal response and trim/dust tone. A quiet lower-body dust gradient is stored as vertex color on the cached painted geometry. This is authored appearance, not measured automotive PBR data. It contains no painted directional light or animated dirt. Glazing retains transparent material semantics for sight queries, while actual glass triangles remain solid to bullets. Civilian lamp lenses have no emission or point lights; the established Gnucci vehicle retains its own factory, lamps and objective identity.

The factory returns `{ group, movementBounds, visualBounds, profile, resources }`. `movementBounds` contains fresh local lower/upper `Box3` values derived from the actual authored part families. The caller transforms copies after placement and registers collision. These boxes remain conservative around tapered glass and wheel openings; projectile queries use the real rendered triangles. Profile metadata includes wheel centers/radii and names that correspond to immutable vertex ranges in each merged geometry's `civilianParts` metadata. Van profiles also expose `glazingRegions` with a pane name, an actual outer-surface probe and an inward direction, plus a `cargo` side probe and its opacity. Tests use these to distinguish transparent openings from opaque cargo panels in the actual rendered geometry. Geometry and materials are shared assets and must not be disposed or edited when removing an individual group.

The original three variant caches retain 1,359,072 bytes of merged position, normal, UV and vertex-color arrays. The current four parked vehicles use those three shapes plus the panel van, retaining 1,820,016 merged bytes. Shared source tire/hub/arch primitives retain another 25,408 bytes, for 1,845,424 bytes total in ordinary play. Replacing one sedan with the panel van adds 36 rendered triangles and no material draws to the four-vehicle workload: 13,788 triangles and 24 draws before culling. It adds one 460,944-byte shape cache because the far sedan still uses the original sedan cache. The four authored vehicles share ten material instances, with zero texture bytes or added lights.

QA previews initialize all five shapes at QA installation, retaining 2,304,720 merged bytes, or 2,330,128 bytes with shared source primitives. The preview groups reuse the installed panel van's paint and finish materials. Counts describe CPU source buffers and one material pass before camera culling; they exclude temporary construction peaks, driver residency, shadow repetitions and browser composition. Full-world counts, source/download sizes and runtime measurements are recorded separately in the vehicle review evidence.

All extrusion, profile fitting, vertex coloring and per-material merging runs during initial construction. Each silhouette is built only once, including when owners select different paint/finish combinations. A fresh vehicle instance allocates its group, mesh nodes and bounds while reusing the cached buffers. No new animation, per-frame geometry work, texture painting, physics step or lighting callback is installed.

The first civilian-car visual review rejected the low nose/tail trial: the stamped face fell away beneath the top corners of the black lamp panel, making the panel read as a separate applied plate. Raising the existing face behind the complete lamp panel and retaining a shallow hood/trunk roll seats the assembly without adding triangles, materials or changing the overall bounds. Focused tests probe the actual body triangles behind the panel corners and center.

The van review rejected a 25 mm open vertical strip between the forward passenger cargo window and the cab's rear post. Actual triangle rays and the window-perimeter test exposed the opening after the first rendered review. Extending the existing pane and its rails into the post gives 25 mm of overlap without changing triangles, batches or bounds. The rejected test output is retained with the matched candidate and final in-game quarter views. The twenty focused asset checks also verify roof/shoulder seating, pillar slopes, mirror contacts, cargo-panel support, window-to-post fit, rear-hinge contact and closed surface topology. Separate integration tests verify ground contact, collision envelopes, projectile and sight semantics. These construction checks complement the in-game views and moving street review; they are not a substitute for them.
