# Stair physics review

The reported need to jump was reproduced in the actual player controller against the built world. At quarter analog input, the second and third flights could cycle at their first riser for 15 seconds: auto-step raised the body, ground support disappeared, and the capsule fell and slid backward. Normal walking and sprinting often completed the ascent but repeatedly became airborne without a jump command. Existing geometry tests mostly assigned a capsule's horizontal velocity directly, which missed the interaction with real player acceleration and ground state.

The step finder raised the body when its leading edge reached the tread, before the centre of the foot was above that tread. On the next frame, the floor resolver required the centre to be inside the surface. It therefore lost support, and subsequent rounded-edge collisions could redirect horizontal movement upward. The new resolver retains support over the circular footprint of a body that was already grounded and is not ascending. It uses the existing 2.5 cm downward probe and never raises support above the previous foot height.

Upward stepping still passes the original 0.30 m riser and full capsule clearance checks. Airborne floor crossings retain the original centre-only rule. Jumps, tall obstacles, low ceilings and unsupported gaps remain physical obstacles; the circular footprint does not invent support at the corners of a padded rectangle. No stair dimensions, rail colliders, landing elevations, invisible ramps, player radius or camera smoothing values changed.

A separate player-side cutoff also disabled automatic stepping for accepted analog magnitudes below approximately 0.032 while continuing to move the body. That second cutoff is removed apart from a negligible numerical epsilon. The input layer's gamepad dead zone remains unchanged, so the change does not introduce controller drift.

The physical review found all 56 treads clear for the standing player along the intended lane and at offsets of ±0.70 m. The entrance route beneath the second flight, all three intermediate turns, the top landing and the rooftop threshold have continuous support and adequate headroom. The doorway retains about 1.999 m above the standing floor/skin, compared with the 1.84 m player body. The narrowest checked passage under the second flight has about 2.219 m. The visible wall handrails and central guards remain the legitimate limits of the walking lanes.

Independent before/after CPU probes covered 48 ascent scenarios and 168 sequential tread stop/restart checks using real `playerUpdate` input and acceleration. Slow analog movement that previously stalled now completes; all tested ascents retained ground contact with no spontaneous upward velocity. Separate shared-solver probes completed 48 NPC flight traversals. These are movement and collision results, not browser frame-rate measurements or controller hardware certification.

The final `npm run check` passes ESLint, **1,045 Node tests**, and the production build. The 56 new real-player tests cover 30 complete ascents (ten keyboard/stick/stance combinations at 30, 60 and 120 Hz), 168 sequential stops/restarts, eight fine-stick starts through the real input dead zone, and six complete descents. They assert capsule clearance on every tick, no synthetic jumps, no lost ascent support and no uncommanded upward velocity. Five additional solver regressions isolate leading-foot support, slow/fast risers, circular corners, airborne behavior and ledge departure. There are no inter-step teleports or forced horizontal velocities in the real-player fixture.

The recorded full-route audit uses 851 world and ammo-box colliders. Walking, sprinting, slow analog input and aiming while crouched each reached the roof with zero airborne ascent frames and zero jumps. Camera position remained finite; the largest observed change was about 4.9 cm in one 120 Hz step during sprinting. This is a simulated movement measurement, not a rendered FPS or visual-quality claim.
